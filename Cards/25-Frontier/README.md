# 25 · Frontier

State each open problem exactly, say what is proved around it and what is not, tell a claim from an accepted proof from a formalisation, and follow a route of earlier cards to the point where the research literature starts. Nothing here promises that reading equips anyone to solve a problem; it promises the reader will know precisely what the problem is.

## 01 · How Research Works · 7 cards

*The habits of research mathematics: proof, conjecture, counterexample, statement, paper, machine, and how to start on an open problem*

1. Proof: a general argument, not a pile of checked cases
2. Kinds of claim: theorem, conjecture, model, approximation, and the status each carries
3. Counterexamples: one failure refutes a universal claim, and a million successes prove nothing
4. Reading a statement: quantifiers, domain, hypotheses, conclusion, and the boundary cases
5. Reading a paper: find the theorem, compare it with the abstract, trace what it depends on
6. Machines in proofs: numerical evidence, symbolic computation, exhaustive search, and proof assistants
7. Working on a problem: small cases, the literature, the status ledger, and knowing when you are wrong

## 02 · The Millennium Problems · 7 cards

*Each problem stated exactly, with its hypotheses, what it does not claim, what is known, and the route that reaches it*

1. The Riemann hypothesis: every nontrivial zero of zeta has real part one half
2. Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer: the rank of an elliptic curve equals the order of vanishing of its L-function
3. P versus NP: is checking a solution really easier than finding one
4. Navier-Stokes: do smooth solutions of the fluid equations exist for all time in three dimensions
5. The Hodge conjecture: which cohomology classes come from algebraic subvarieties
6. Yang-Mills existence and mass gap: build the quantum theory rigorously and show its lightest particle has positive mass
7. The Poincare conjecture, solved: a simply connected closed three-manifold is a sphere

## 03 · Primes and Numbers · 7 cards

*The open and recently settled questions about primes and integers, and the ones with prizes attached*

1. Twin primes: infinitely many primes two apart, and the bounded gap of 246 that is proved
2. Goldbach: every even number above 2 is a sum of two primes
3. Landau's four problems: Goldbach, twin primes, Legendre, and primes of the form n squared plus 1
4. The abc conjecture: when a plus b equals c, the radical of abc is rarely much smaller than c
5. Beal's conjecture: A to the x plus B to the y equals C to the z with exponents above 2 forces a common factor
6. Fermat's last theorem, proved in 1995 and reported formalised in 2026: what a formalisation adds
7. Erdos problems: a database of hundreds, with prizes, and the ones a computer search can touch

## 04 · Geometry and Combinatorics · 6 cards

*Open problems you can draw, some recently moved by computer search*

1. The unit-distance problem: how many pairs of n points can be exactly one apart
2. Kakeya: a set containing a unit segment in every direction, and how small it can be
3. Colouring the plane: how many colours so that no two points at distance one share a colour
4. Cycle double cover: every bridgeless graph has cycles covering each edge exactly twice
5. Sunflowers and union-closed families: two set-system problems with recent progress
6. Sphere packing, solved in dimensions 8 and 24: Viazovska's modular-form proof

## 05 · Analysis and Dynamics · 6 cards

*Open questions about equations and iterations, including the 2026 Navier-Stokes claim*

1. The September 2026 Navier-Stokes claim: forced finite-time blow-up for versions C and D
2. Collatz: halve if even, triple and add one if odd, and always reach 1
3. The August 2026 zeta-zeros claim: a reported rise in the proportion of zeros on the line from 41.6 to 67.2 percent
4. The Jacobian conjecture: a polynomial map with constant nonzero Jacobian has a polynomial inverse
5. Two algebra questions: is every group sofic, and is the sum of two nil left ideals nil (Kothe)
6. Percolation: when does a random lattice connect, and the reported 'dying percolation' result to verify

## 06 · Algebra, Logic and Computation · 5 cards

*Open problems where algebra meets logic and computation*

1. The inverse Galois problem: is every finite group the Galois group of some extension of the rationals
2. The continuum hypothesis: no size between the integers and the reals, and why it cannot be settled
3. The unique games conjecture: one hardness assumption that would settle many approximation questions
4. Graph isomorphism: are two drawings the same graph, and Babai's quasipolynomial algorithm
5. Hilbert's and Smale's lists: the problems that shaped a century, and the ones still open
