# 18 · Functional analysis

Work in infinite-dimensional spaces as steadily as in ordinary space: measure size with a norm, rely on completeness, name the dual, and read an operator through its spectrum. Differentiate kinks and spikes, pose an equation weakly in a Sobolev space, and turn a generator into an evolution that runs forward in time.

## 01 · Normed and Banach Spaces · 10 cards

*length and completeness in spaces of sequences and functions; the three Banach theorems; Hahn-Banach and the dual space*

1. Normed and Banach spaces: giving a length to functions so limits cannot escape
2. Sequence spaces: infinite lists of numbers with a size rule
3. Function spaces C and Lp: two ways to size a function, two different worlds
4. Equivalent norms: in finitely many dimensions every length rule agrees
5. Infinite dimensions: the unit ball stops being compact and bases get subtler
6. Hahn-Banach: a measurement on part of a space extends to all of it, no bigger
7. Separation: two convex sets that miss each other can be split by a flat wall
8. Three Banach theorems: completeness turns weak checks into full continuity
9. Dual spaces: the continuous measurements on a space, and what they turn out to be
10. Reflexive spaces: when a space is exactly the dual of its own dual

## 02 · Hilbert Spaces · 9 cards

*inner products and angles; projection; orthonormal expansions and Parseval; representation of functionals; kernels; weak limits*

1. Inner products: length and angle in a space of signals
2. Projection: every point has exactly one nearest point in a closed subspace
3. Orthonormal bases: every signal is an infinite sum of perpendicular pieces
4. Completeness of sines and cosines: Fourier series leave nothing behind
5. Orthogonal polynomials: straightening the powers into a perpendicular family
6. Riesz representation: every measurement on a Hilbert space is one fixed vector
7. Lax-Milgram: a bounded energy form that stays away from zero has one solution
8. Reproducing kernels: a space where reading off a value is an inner product
9. Weak convergence: only the measurements must settle, and that buys compactness

## 03 · Bounded Operators · 10 cards

*operator norm and continuity; the model operators; adjoints; compactness; spectrum and resolvent; the two spectral theorems*

1. Bounded operators: for a linear map, no blow-up is the same as continuity
2. Three model operators: integrate against a kernel, multiply, shift
3. Adjoints: the mirror operator, and the classes it singles out
4. Plancherel: the Fourier transform is a rigid rotation of the space of signals
5. Compact operators: the maps that still behave like finite matrices
6. Neumann series: a geometric series that inverts an operator near the identity
7. Spectrum: the numbers at which an operator cannot be inverted
8. Compact and symmetric: an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors, eigenvalues dying away
9. Fredholm alternative: either always solvable, or finitely many obstructions
10. Bounded and symmetric: diagonal against a family of projections, not eigenvectors

## 04 · Distributions and Sobolev Spaces · 10 cards

*test functions and distributions; delta and jumps; convolution and mollifiers; tempered transforms; weak derivatives and Sobolev estimates*

1. Distributions: objects defined only by what they read on smooth probes
2. The delta: a spike of unit mass, and the derivative of a switch
3. Convolution and mollifiers: smoothing a rough object without moving it
4. Fundamental solution: solve once for a spike, then convolve for any load
5. Tempered distributions: taking the Fourier transform of a hum or a spike
6. Sampling: a row of spikes explains why evenly spaced samples suffice
7. Weak derivatives: differentiating a kink, and the spaces that keep score
8. Weak solutions: asking an equation to hold only against smooth probes
9. Sobolev and Poincare: trading derivative control for size and smoothness
10. Boundary values and compactness: the two Sobolev results existence proofs lean on

## 05 · Unbounded Operators and Semigroups · 9 cards

*domains, closedness and self-adjointness; semigroups and their generators; forcing, splitting, and probabilistic evolutions*

1. Unbounded operators: differentiation only works where you let it
2. Self-adjoint extensions: symmetric is not enough, the domain decides
3. Evolution as an equation: rate of change equals an operator times the state
4. Semigroups: a family of operators that composes like elapsed time
5. Hille-Yosida: exactly which operators drive a well-behaved evolution
6. Duhamel: add up each nudge, carried forward by the unforced flow
7. Splitting: alternate two evolutions in tiny steps to get their combination
8. Feller semigroups: the evolution of averages along a random path
9. Two evolutions: heat smooths at once, waves only rotate

*multiplying in a complete space; the Gelfand picture and C-star algebras; fixed points; extreme points and convex duality*
