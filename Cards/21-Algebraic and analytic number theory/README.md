# 21 · Algebraic and analytic number theory

Prove the prime number theorem with an error term, state the Riemann hypothesis precisely and say exactly what is proved around it, follow Dirichlet's theorem and the sieve results, and do Galois theory. Compute in a ring of algebraic integers and read the statements of Fermat, Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer and abc as mathematics rather than folklore.

7. Squarefree numbers: why six in every ten integers are divisible by no square
8. Chebyshev's bounds: trapping the prime count between two multiples of x over log x
9. Mertens' theorems: the reciprocals of the primes add up like log log x

9. The Riemann hypothesis: the exact statement, and three equivalents worth knowing
10. Zeros on the line: what is proved, from Hardy to a positive proportion

*Characters as periodic multiplicative patterns, their L-functions, primes in progressions, Gauss sums and quadratic reciprocity*

1. Dirichlet characters: periodic multiplicative patterns that pick out one remainder class
2. Quadratic residues: which numbers are squares on a prime clock, and Euler's test
3. Dirichlet L-functions: one series per character, each with its own Euler product
4. Dirichlet's theorem: a progression coprime to its step holds infinitely many primes
5. Siegel-Walfisz: the prime count in a progression, uniform over small moduli
6. Gauss sums: weighted sums over roots of unity, with a size you can pin down exactly
7. Quadratic reciprocity: swapping the two primes, proved with a Gauss sum
8. The Jacobi symbol: extending the symbol to odd moduli so it computes without factoring
